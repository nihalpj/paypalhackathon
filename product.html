The problem: India's compliance gauntlet

OPGSP → PA-CB regime shift

RBI replaced the OPGSP framework (31 Oct 2023 circular, consolidated 15 Sep 2025). Cross-border aggregators now answer directly to the RBI with a ₹25 lakh per-unit cap — and the documentation burden was pushed downstream onto every freelancer and SMB exporter.

FEMA 2026: the 9-month clock

FEMA 23(R)/2026-RB (in force 1 Oct 2026) compresses export realization to 9 months (12 for INR/Vostro invoicing). Miss it and face penalties up to 3× the sum involved plus ₹5,000/day — and a place on the RBI Caution List.

eBRC self-certification

Since Trade Notice 33/2023-24, banks push Inward Remittance Messages to DGFT and exporters must match and self-certify eBRCs themselves — with a mandatory GATS "Mode of Export of Services" field since 1 May 2025 and GST invoice details mandatory since 13 Jan 2026.

The purpose-code minefield

P0802 vs P0803 vs P0807 (SOFTEX/Non-STP obligations) vs the deadly P0103: misclassify a service export as an advance for physical goods and EDPMS opens a "ghost" shipment obligation that can never be closed. GST Rule 96A adds 18% interest if LUT exports aren't realized in forex within a year.

The core injustice: a freelance developer in Indore who invoices $500 to a client in Berlin has the same regulatory obligations as a ₹5,000-crore exporter — FEMA realization tracking, EDPMS closure, DGFT self-certification, LUT accountability. Big exporters hire compliance teams. Freelancers get a government portal, a 9-month clock, and penalties that start at 3× the payment. AutoFIRC exists because the obligation scales down to one person, but the tooling never did.

The DGFT portal: what must be done after every payment — and why

What the DGFT eBRC system is

The DGFT (Directorate General of Foreign Trade, Ministry of Commerce) runs India's eBRC platform — the national registry where every foreign remittance an exporter receives must be reconciled against the export it paid for. Your bank reports the money in; you must prove what it was for. The eBRC (electronic Bank Realization Certificate) is that proof.

Why it exists — the regulatory chain

FEMA obliges every export earning to be realized, repatriated, and traced. RBI's EDPMS database tracks each export invoice as an open obligation until a matching inward remittance closes it; the eBRC is the certificate that performs that closure. Until it is issued, your LUT zero-rating under GST Rule 96A is unproven, your bank keeps flagging the entry, and the FEMA clock keeps running.

The manual gauntlet — today's DGFT website workflow

  1. Bank Your foreign client pays; the bank converts to INR, credits you, and — days later, with no notification — pushes an IRM (Inward Remittance Message) to the DGFT repository against your IEC.
  2. Merchant Log into dgft.gov.in with IEC-linked credentials, open the eBRC module, and periodically hunt for new unmapped IRMs — there is no alert when one arrives.
  3. Merchant Manually match each IRM (remitter name, foreign amount, currency, date) against your own invoices and PayPal receipts — a spreadsheet exercise repeated for every client payment.
  4. Merchant "Club" IRMs to invoices under DGFT rules: only remittances sharing bank, account and currency may be clubbed, and advance payments require the invoice date to be on/after the remittance date. Club wrong and the filing is rejected.
  5. Merchant Complete the self-certification form per invoice: GST invoice number, date and value (mandatory since 13 Jan 2026), SAC code, and the GATS Mode of Export of Services 1–4 (mandatory since 1 May 2025) — fields most freelancers have never heard of, with no inline guidance.
  6. DGFT On submission, DGFT issues the eBRC number. The exporter must then download the certificate and deliver it to their AD bank so the corresponding EDPMS entry can be closed.
  7. Merchant Repeat for every payment, every month, forever. Miss one IRM and it silently ages into an open EDPMS obligation against your IEC.

What happens if you don't

  • Open EDPMS entries age → RBI Caution List → banks stop processing your export collections entirely
  • LUT exports not realized in forex within the allowed period → GST payable plus 18% interest (Rule 96A)
  • FEMA 23(R)/2026-RB breach → penalties up to 3× the sum involved + ₹5,000/day of continuing default
  • Wrong purpose/mode codes create permanent compliance debt (the P0103 "ghost shipment" trap)

What AutoFIRC does instead

  • Production-faithful DGFT Open API client: API key + PBKDF2-hashed client secret (65,536 iterations), every eBRC payload RSA-SHA256 signed — portal steps 2–6 become one automated pass per payment
  • Clubbing rules encoded deterministically in autofirc-core — auditable, never improvised by the LLM
  • Fallback KERNEL cloud-browser agent drives the real DGFT portal UI with a 24fps live view and full replay for auditors
  • This demo runs against a high-fidelity mock DGFT on Render — the real API requires an IEC, IP whitelisting and FIU registration that can't be obtained during a hackathon; the request/response contract is identical

The solution: four autonomous phases

PHASE 1

Ingestion

Signed PayPal webhooks verified offline (raw-byte CRC32 + RSA-SHA256, paypal.com cert allowlist), acknowledged in under 2 seconds, and queued for async processing. Idempotent by webhook ID.

PHASE 2

AI Analysis & Documents

A guardrailed LLM assigns the RBI purpose code, GATS export mode, and SAC code with a confidence score — P0103 is hard-forbidden for services. GST Rule 46 invoices and LUT declarations are rendered as PDFs and stored with signed URLs. Low confidence routes to manual review.

PHASE 3

Dashboard & Command

An AG Studio compliance ledger with FEMA deadline widgets (amber at 7 months, red at 8) and a conversational agent bar: "Isolate October transactions without eBRC under P0802 and stage them."

PHASE 4

Petition & Reconciliation

The compliance packet (invoice + LUT + PayPal receipt as FIRA) is dispatched to the bank via Zapier MCP. AutoFIRC then authenticates to DGFT (PBKDF2 + RSA-SHA256), matches IRMs under clubbing rules, and self-certifies the eBRC — or drives the DGFT portal itself through a KERNEL browser agent.

Demo note: the real DGFT Open API requires an IEC, IP whitelisting, and FIU registration — unobtainable in a hackathon. AutoFIRC ships a production-faithful client plus a high-fidelity mock DGFT server, and a KERNEL cloud-browser agent that operates the real portal UI with live session replay.

Detailed flow — 15 steps, 5 actors

  1. Merchant Finishes a milestone; AutoFIRC issues a draft invoice via PayPal Invoicing v2 with detailed line items and sends it.
  2. Foreign Client Pays on the PayPal-hosted page in local currency; PayPal handles FX inside the PA-CB banking channel and settles INR.
  3. PayPal Emits PAYMENT.CAPTURE.COMPLETED → AutoFIRC buffers raw bytes, verifies CRC32 + RSA-SHA256 offline (cert must be paypal.com), inserts a ledger row, enqueues a job, returns HTTP 200 in < 2s.
  4. AutoFIRC The row appears on the dashboard as Pending Analysis in real time.
  5. AutoFIRC The worker fetches OAuth2 token → GET /v2/checkout/orders/{id} + GET /v2/payments/captures/{id} for line items, buyer metadata, INR settlement, FX rate, fees.
  6. AutoFIRC The guardrailed classifier returns {purpose_code, mode_of_export, sac_code, confidence_score}. P0103 forbidden for services; remote digital services → Mode 1 (Cross-Border Supply); P0807 flags a SOFTEX/Non-STP warning.
  7. AutoFIRC Confidence < 85 → Manual Review. Purpose codes are never hand-editable (TBML guardrail).
  8. AutoFIRC The PDF engine renders the GST Rule 46 commercial invoice (17 mandatory fields + LUT endorsement) and the LUT declaration → Supabase Storage with signed URLs → Compliance Packet Ready.
  9. Merchant Opens the AG Studio ledger: FEMA countdown widgets, eBRC status tiles, purpose-code charts. Amber at 7 months, red at 8.
  10. Merchant Types a natural-language command; the Studio Agent Framework isolates the rows; merchant clicks Authorize Bank Petition (bulk).
  11. Bank Receives the encrypted packet (invoice + LUT + PayPal capture receipt as FIRA) via Zapier MCP email; verifies and closes the EDPMS entry; IRMs flow to DGFT.
  12. DGFT AutoFIRC authenticates (API key + PBKDF2-hashed client secret, 65,536 iterations) and fetches pending IRMs for the merchant's IEC.
  13. AutoFIRC IRMs are matched under clubbing rules (same bank, account, currency; advances need invoice date ≥ remittance date).
  14. DGFT The RSA-SHA256-signed payload (invoice + SAC + Mode of Export + GST details) is submitted → eBRC number issued → Fully Reconciled & Compliant. Failures retry with backoff.
  15. AutoFIRC If the Open API is unreachable, a KERNEL cloud-browser agent logs into the DGFT portal and self-certifies via the UI — with a 24fps live view and full replay for auditors.

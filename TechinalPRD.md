Product Requirements Document (PRD): AutoFIRC - The Cross-Border Compliance Agent (v2.0)Executive SummaryThe architecture of cross-border remittances for digital service exporters in emerging markets is currently undergoing a profound regulatory metamorphosis. Driven by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI), the Directorate General of Foreign Trade (DGFT), and the Goods and Services Tax (GST) Council, the compliance burden has shifted dramatically from institutional banks directly to payment intermediaries and individual merchants. AutoFIRC (v2.0) is conceptualized as an autonomous compliance agent architected to seamlessly bridge the gap between PayPal’s global cross-border payment rails and these stringent, highly localized regulatory frameworks. By leveraging advanced artificial intelligence to monitor incoming transaction webhooks, accurately categorize transaction intent against statutory purpose codes, and dynamically generate requisite local banking and taxation documentation, AutoFIRC transforms a heavily manual, error-prone reconciliation process into a zero-touch, agentic workflow. The system fundamentally shifts the paradigm from reactive compliance to proactive, AI-driven regulatory orchestration, ensuring seamless foreign exchange realization for small and medium businesses (SMBs) and freelancers without the administrative friction that traditionally paralyzes global digital trade.Regulatory Landscape and Problem StatementFreelancers, startups, and digital service exporters in India face severe administrative friction when receiving international remittances. To remain compliant with overlapping local regulations, merchants cannot simply receive funds into their accounts and deploy them as working capital. They are mandated by law to manually reconcile inward international remittances against specific commercial invoices, generate GST-compliant export documentation under a Letter of Undertaking (LUT), and petition their local Authorised Dealer (AD) Category-I banks for Foreign Inward Remittance Certificates (FIRCs) or Advices (FIRAs). The regulatory environment governing these processes has recently experienced unprecedented structural shifts that compound this complexity.The Transition from OPGSP to PA-CBHistorically, Indian exporters receiving low-value cross-border payments relied on the Online Payment Gateway Service Provider (OPGSP) framework. Under OPGSP, the AD Category-I bank held the primary regulatory relationship and authorization, allowing fintech platforms to operate via standing arrangements with these banks. This model effectively shielded the exporter from direct regulatory scrutiny, as the bank handled the bulk of the reporting. However, citing the need for direct oversight as digital export volumes surged, the RBI repealed the OPGSP guidelines entirely. The framework in force today is the Reserve Bank of India (Regulation of Payment Aggregators) Directions, which introduced the Payment Aggregator - Cross Border (PA-CB) regime.Under the PA-CB framework, non-bank fintech intermediaries must hold direct RBI authorization, register with the Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU-IND), and maintain a minimum net worth of ₹15 crore at application, scaling to ₹25 crore. Crucially, the PA-CB rules enforce a strict transaction ceiling of ₹25 lakh per unit for both inward and outward flows, and mandate the segregation of funds into distinct Inward Collection Accounts (InCA) and Outward Collection Accounts (OCA) held with AD banks. Because the PA-CB entity now answers directly to the RBI, the compliance burden has been pushed downstream. The merchant must provide flawless documentation to both the PA-CB and the AD bank to prove the legitimacy of the export, ensuring that the funds are not delayed or returned.FeatureLegacy OPGSP FrameworkCurrent PA-CB FrameworkRegulatory BasisA.P. Effective October 1, 2026, these regulations fundamentally alter the timelines and mechanisms for export realization. The statutory window for realizing and repatriating export proceeds has been compressed from the legacy 15-month period down to just 9 months from the date of shipment or the date of the invoice for services. A slightly extended 12-month window applies if the export is invoiced or settled in Indian Rupees via Vostro accounts.The RBI tracks every export transaction through the Export Data Processing and Monitoring System (EDPMS). When a shipping bill or service invoice is generated, an entry is opened. It remains open until the AD bank matches the inward remittance to the specific document and closes the loop. Failure to realize funds within the accelerated 9-month timeframe exposes the merchant to severe consequences under Section 13 of FEMA. Penalties can reach up to three times the sum involved for quantifiable contraventions, alongside an additional daily penalty of ₹5,000 for continuing violations. More critically, prolonged non-realization results in the exporter being placed on the RBI's Caution List, which prevents future exports unless they are backed by full advance payments or irrevocable Letters of Credit.DGFT eBRC Modernization and API IntegrationTo prove that export proceeds have been realized, exporters require a Bank Realisation Certificate (BRC). The Directorate General of Foreign Trade (DGFT) has recently overhauled this process, moving away from a system where banks manually issued these certificates. Under Trade Notice No. 33/2023-24, banks now transmit Inward Remittance Messages (IRMs) directly to the DGFT portal electronically. The burden of certification now falls entirely on the exporter, who must log into the DGFT system, match the IRMs to their respective shipping bills or commercial invoices, and self-certify the electronic BRC (eBRC).This self-certification process is heavily rules-based. For instance, IRMs can only be clubbed together to form a single eBRC if they originate from the same bank, share the same bank account number, and are denominated in the same currency. Furthermore, DGFT Trade Notice No. 02/2025-26 introduced a new mandatory requirement for service exporters effective May 2025: the inclusion of a "Mode of Export of Services" field. Exporters must now categorize their services according to the General Agreement on Trade in Services (GATS) framework, selecting between Mode 1 (Cross-Border Supply, such as remote IT consulting), Mode 2 (Consumption Abroad), Mode 3 (Commercial Presence), or Mode 4 (Presence of Natural Persons).To facilitate high-volume processing, the DGFT introduced an Open API framework (Trade Notice No. 12/2024-25), allowing exporters to integrate their Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) systems directly with the eBRC repository. However, this API requires a sophisticated cryptographic handshake, involving IP whitelisting, PBKDF2 hashed client secrets, and payload encryption via RSA-SHA256 digital signatures. For an SMB without a dedicated engineering team, implementing this API to close out EDPMS entries is virtually impossible.GST Rule 96A and Letter of Undertaking (LUT) ExposuresOn the domestic taxation front, the export of services is considered a "zero-rated supply" under Section 16 of the Integrated Goods and Services Tax (IGST) Act. To export without the upfront payment of IGST, merchants must file a Letter of Undertaking (LUT) via Form GST RFD-11. However, this exemption is strictly conditional under Rule 96A of the Central Goods and Services Tax (CGST) Rules.Rule 96A mandates that the payment for exported services must be received in convertible foreign exchange within one year from the date of the invoice. If the foreign client fails to pay, or if the remittance is held up in the banking system and not reconciled via a FIRC/eBRC within this one-year window, the exporter is legally deemed to have defaulted. Upon default, the exporter must pay the applicable IGST that was originally exempted, plus an aggressive 18 percent annual interest under Section 50 of the CGST Act, within 15 days of the expiry of the one-year period.The Complexities of RBI Purpose Codes and STPICompounding the reconciliation challenge is the strict enforcement of RBI purpose codes from the Foreign Exchange Transactions Electronic Reporting System (FETERS) directory. Every incoming cross-border transaction must be tagged with a specific alphanumeric code indicating the economic nature of the remittance. For digital service exporters, selecting the correct code is a compliance minefield.For instance, payments for software consulting are typically tagged as P0802 (Software consultancy/implementation), while payments for database management fall under P0803. A critical distinction exists for P0807 (Off-site Software Exports). The P0807 code has historically been intertwined with the requirement to file SOFTEX forms through the Software Technology Parks of India (STPI). Entities engaging in off-site software exports are required to obtain Non-STP registration, which involves annual service charges based on export turnover and stringent quarterly performance reporting. Using P0807 without holding Non-STP registration and filing the corresponding SOFTEX declarations triggers immediate regulatory scrutiny.The most dangerous compliance error a digital exporter can make is misclassifying a service export as an advance receipt for physical goods using purpose code P0103. Code P0103 is strictly mandated for advance payments against physical export contracts that require eventual customs clearance and a physical Shipping Bill. If an IT freelancer mistakenly accepts a payment tagged with P0103, the RBI's EDPMS system registers a "ghost" export obligation, expecting physical goods to depart an Indian port. Because no physical shipment will ever occur, the transaction cannot be regularized, leaving the exporter trapped in an unfulfillable compliance state that ultimately leads to placement on the Caution List.AutoFIRC is engineered to navigate this entire gauntlet. The workflow is segregated into four distinct operational phases.Phase 1: Transaction and IngestionThe lifecycle commences when the Merchant finalizes a project milestone and issues a checkout link or draft invoice to the Foreign Client using the PayPal Invoicing v2 API. The Foreign Client authorizes the payment in their local currency. PayPal processes the transaction, handles the complex currency conversion required by the PA-CB framework (ensuring the foreign exchange leg stays inside the banking channel), and settles the funds into the Merchant's account.Upon successful capture, the PayPal system emits a PAYMENT.CAPTURE.COMPLETED webhook to the merchant's registered listener URL. The AutoFIRC backend, deployed on the Next.js App Router, exposes a secure POST route handler specifically designed to intercept this payload. Because PayPal's webhook delivery mechanism requires a strict HTTP 200 OK response within a highly constrained time window to prevent exponential backoff retries (up to 25 retries over 3 days), the ingestion layer is decoupled from the heavy processing layer. The Next.js route handler utilizes Upstash QStash to immediately push the raw webhook payload into a serverless background message queue, subsequently returning a success response to PayPal.Crucially, the ingestion phase handles rigorous cryptographic verification. PayPal signs all webhook payloads using RSA-SHA256 asymmetric signature verification. The AutoFIRC route handler must extract the raw byte stream of the incoming request before any JSON body parsing occurs. Standard Express or Next.js body parsers alter the character encoding, which inevitably corrupts the checksum. The system computes an unsigned 32-bit cyclic redundancy check (CRC32) of this raw body using the exact bytes received. It then constructs a pipe-delimited string formatted exactly as transmission_id|transmission_time|webhook_id|crc32, utilizing the developer's registered PAYPAL_WEBHOOK_ID stored in the environment variables. This string is cryptographically verified against the base64-encoded PAYPAL-TRANSMISSION-SIG using the X.509 public key fetched from the PAYPAL-CERT-URL header. The system strictly validates that the certificate URL belongs to the trusted paypal.com domain to prevent spoofing. Once validated locally without relying on the latency-heavy postback verification API, the transaction ID is logged into the PostgreSQL relational database, transitioning the record status to "Pending Analysis."Phase 2: Contextual Analysis and Document GenerationTriggered by the QStash worker retrieving the queued message, the AutoFIRC Agent begins the contextual analysis phase. The agent first requires granular Level 2 and Level 3 line-item data, such as specific service descriptions, tax breakdowns, and buyer metadata, which is often absent or truncated in the primary webhook payload. To resolve this, the system utilizes the @paypal/agent-toolkit, specifically invoking the get_order tool to securely authenticate with the PayPal REST API and retrieve the comprehensive transaction schema.This enriched payload is then passed to the Vercel AI SDK. Using the generateObject function coupled with a strictly typed Zod schema, the underlying LLM (such as Anthropic Claude 3.5 Sonnet or OpenAI GPT-4o) is instructed to classify the transaction against the RBI's purpose code directory. The AI must perform complex semantic reasoning to distinguish between nuanced service types. For instance, it must evaluate whether the service constitutes "Business and management consultancy" (P1006) or "Software consultancy/implementation" (P0802). The system prompt explicitly constrains the LLM with severe negative reinforcement against selecting purpose code P0103 for any transaction lacking physical shipping attributes, preventing the creation of unfulfillable EDPMS obligations.Furthermore, to comply with the new mandates of DGFT Trade Notice No. 02/2025-26, the generateObject schema requires the LLM to determine the "Mode of Export of Services". Based on the invoicing data, the agent autonomously assigns "Mode 1" (Cross-Border Supply) for remote software development or consulting.Once the AI securely returns the structured JSON containing the validated purpose code, the Mode of Export, the corresponding Service Accounting Code (SAC), and a calculated confidence score, the document generation protocol initiates. Utilizing @react-pdf/renderer operating server-side within the Next.js App Router, AutoFIRC dynamically compiles a commercial invoice and a Letter of Undertaking (LUT) declaration. The generated invoice strictly adheres to the 17 mandatory fields dictated by GST Rule 46, ensuring the inclusion of the supplier's GSTIN, consecutive serial numbers, correct SAC codes, and the specific statutory endorsement required for LUT exports ("Supply meant for export under bond or letter of undertaking without payment of integrated tax"). The generated PDF documents are streamed directly into an encrypted Supabase Storage bucket. The system assigns publicly inaccessible, signed URLs to these objects and appends them to the database ledger, shifting the transaction status to "Compliance Packet Ready."Phase 3: Dashboard Interaction and Agentic CommandThe Merchant interfaces with the system via a high-performance compliance ledger powered by AG Grid. The grid renders real-time data fetched from the PostgreSQL database, displaying comprehensive columns for transaction dates, foreign client names, INR settlement values, AI-assigned purpose codes, GST LUT validity statuses, and critically, the FEMA realization deadlines. To ensure compliance with the compressed 9-month realization window established by FEMA 23(R)/2026-RB (or 12 months for INR settlements), the grid features dynamic conditional formatting. It visually flags rows in amber at the 7-month mark and red at the 8-month mark for any transaction lacking successful eBRC reconciliation.To abstract the complexity of data manipulation, AutoFIRC implements a conversational chat interface leveraging the AG Studio Agent Framework. Rather than forcing the user to manually apply complex column filters and sorting logic, the Merchant can submit natural language prompts. For example, the Merchant types: "Isolate all transactions from October that lack an eBRC, fall under purpose code P0802, and stage them for bulk DGFT certification." The integrated agent interprets this prompt, dynamically constructs the required TypeScript FilterModel configurations, and applies them to the AG Grid API to instantly isolate the relevant records. The Merchant reviews the staged compliance packets and clicks the "Authorize Bank Petition" button to execute the downstream workflow in bulk.Phase 4: Bank Petition, DGFT API, and ReconciliationUpon authorization, the system executes a dual-pronged reconciliation process designed to satisfy both the AD bank and the DGFT. First, it bundles the AI-generated Commercial Invoices, LUT declarations, and the PayPal settlement receipts (acting as the Foreign Inward Remittance Advice or FIRA) into a secure, encrypted email payload. The agent dispatches this packet to the designated Bank Compliance Officer's intake inbox, allowing the AD bank to verify the bona fides of the transaction and officially close the open entry in the RBI's EDPMS system.Secondly, AutoFIRC interfaces directly with the DGFT's Open API for electronic Bank Realisation Certificate (eBRC) self-certification. As mandated by DGFT Trade Notice 33/2023-24, banks transmit Inward Remittance Messages (IRMs) to the DGFT, and exporters must match these against their invoices. AutoFIRC connects to the DGFT REST API to fetch pending IRMs linked to the merchant's Importer Exporter Code (IEC). This connection utilizes a highly secure cryptographic handshake. The system generates an access token by passing an API key, a client ID, and a client secret that is salted with 32 random bytes using SHA-256 and encrypted via PBKDF2 with 256-bit key size and 65,536 iterations.The AutoFIRC Agent evaluates the fetched IRMs against the Merchant's local ledger, applying strict DGFT clubbing rules. It ensures that multiple IRMs are only clubbed if they share the exact currency, bank name, and account number. It also validates that for any advance payments, the invoice date is recorded as greater than or equal to the remittance date. The agent then constructs a JSON payload containing the matched invoice data, the SAC codes, and the newly mandated "Mode of Export of Services" parameter. The payload is digitally signed using an RSA-SHA256 private key and submitted to the DGFT endpoint. Upon receiving a successful acknowledgment and the issued eBRC number from the DGFT API, the AG Grid ledger updates the transaction status to "Fully Reconciled & Compliant," closing the compliance loop.Functional Requirements5.1 Payment Integration and Webhook SecurityRequirement 5.1.1: The Next.js application must expose a dedicated POST endpoint (e.g., app/api/webhooks/paypal/route.ts) strictly for ingesting PAYMENT.CAPTURE.COMPLETED webhooks from the PayPal Sandbox and Production environments.
Requirement 5.1.2: The webhook handler must implement offline self-cryptographic verification to minimize latency. The system must buffer the raw byte stream to calculate an unsigned CRC32 hash, construct the transmission_id|transmission_time|webhook_id|crc32 pipe-delimited string, and verify the RSA-SHA256 signature using the X.509 certificate hosted at the PAYPAL-CERT-URL.
Requirement 5.1.3: The endpoint must instantly return an HTTP 200 OK upon successful cryptographic verification and delegate the JSON payload to an Upstash QStash topic for asynchronous processing, thereby preventing PayPal from initiating its exponential backoff retry cycle.
Requirement 5.1.4: The system must securely authenticate with the PayPal REST API using OAuth2 client credentials to fetch order details. Integration must utilize APIMatic-generated context plugins and TypeScript SDKs for the PayPal Invoicing v2 API. This ensures strict schema adherence during the creation of draft invoices and eliminates the risk of LLM hallucinations regarding API property constraints.5.2 AI Orchestration and Classification EngineRequirement 5.2.1: The system must utilize the Vercel AI SDK to orchestrate inference calls to the underlying large language model (e.g., OpenAI GPT-4o or Anthropic Claude 3.5 Sonnet).
Requirement 5.2.2: The classification module must invoke the generateObject function, supplying a highly constrained Zod schema. The schema must enforce the output of valid JSON containing: purpose_code (string), mode_of_export (integer 1-4), sac_code (string), and confidence_score (number 0-100).
Requirement 5.2.3: The system prompt must contain strict regulatory guardrails. It must explicitly instruct the model that purpose code P0103 is strictly prohibited for service exports, directing it instead towards P0802 (Software consultancy), P0803 (Data processing), or P0807 (Off-site software exports) based on the presence of software development nomenclature. It must also enforce that digital remote services map exclusively to "Mode 1: Cross-Border Supply" to satisfy DGFT Trade Notice No. 02/2025-26.5.3 Document Generation and StorageRequirement 5.3.1: The application must utilize @react-pdf/renderer operating entirely within Next.js server-side route handlers to generate static PDF documents (Commercial Invoices and LUT Declarations) populated with the dynamic JSON data validated by the AI engine.
Requirement 5.3.2: The generated commercial invoice must strictly adhere to the 17 mandatory fields dictated by GST Rule 46, ensuring the inclusion of the supplier's GSTIN, a unique consecutive serial number, the correct SAC code, and the specific endorsement stating that the supply is meant for export under LUT without payment of integrated tax.
Requirement 5.3.3: All generated PDFs must be instantly uploaded to a secure Supabase Storage bucket. Access to these documents must be restricted via Signed URLs with finite expiration windows to maintain data privacy and prevent the unauthorized public exposure of highly sensitive financial records.5.4 Frontend Compliance Ledger and DGFT API IntegrationRequirement 5.4.1: The merchant dashboard must implement AG Grid Enterprise to render high-performance, real-time transaction data fetched from the PostgreSQL database, providing advanced sorting, filtering, and pagination capabilities.
Requirement 5.4.2: The UI must feature an integrated AG Studio Agent conversational interface, enabling the merchant to type natural language queries that the agent translates into dynamic TypeScript FilterModel configurations to manipulate the grid state instantly.
Requirement 5.4.3: The system must track the 9-month realization deadline mandated by FEMA 23(R)/2026-RB. The grid must implement conditional formatting to visually highlight rows for transactions approaching this statutory limit.
Requirement 5.4.4: For automated eBRC generation, the backend must implement the DGFT Open API cryptographic handshake. This includes generating access tokens using PBKDF2 hashing of the client secret and applying RSA-SHA256 digital signatures to the request payloads, enabling the secure transmission of matched invoice and IRM data to the government portal.Non-Functional Requirements6.1 Security and Data PrivacyAll sensitive merchant data, including PayPal API client secrets, DGFT API credentials, and PostgreSQL database records, must be encrypted at rest using AES-256 encryption. The system must strictly enforce transport layer security (TLS 1.3) for all external API communications. Given the severe consequences of Trade-Based Money Laundering (TBML) and FEMA non-compliance, the architectural security posture must prevent any unauthorized modification of AI-assigned purpose codes by the merchant prior to bank submission. Furthermore, the webhook listener must meticulously validate that the certificate fetched during signature verification explicitly matches the trusted paypal.com domain, preventing man-in-the-middle spoofing attacks.6.2 Performance and LatencyThe decoupling of webhook ingestion from AI processing is mission-critical. The initial POST route handler must perform the RSA-SHA256 signature verification and push the payload to the Upstash QStash queue within a strict 2-second maximum latency budget. The subsequent generateObject LLM inference and server-side PDF generation steps, which may collectively take between 4 to 12 seconds, must operate entirely asynchronously within the worker queues, ensuring the PayPal API is never blocked or forced into a retry cycle.6.3 Reliability and IdempotencyBecause PayPal may occasionally transmit duplicate webhook events due to network anomalies or internal simulator retries, the AutoFIRC database must enforce strict idempotency. The system must utilize the unique id field from the webhook payload as a primary key constraint in the PostgreSQL ledger. If a duplicate event is processed by the QStash worker, the system must silently acknowledge the event without triggering duplicate LLM inferences or generating redundant PDF documents, thereby preventing ledger pollution, avoiding duplicate DGFT API calls, and minimizing unnecessary AI inference costs. The system must gracefully handle LLM rate limits and unexpected outputs by falling back to a "Manual Review" status in the merchant dashboard if the AI classification confidence score drops below the 85% threshold.Technical Stack ArchitectureThe implementation leverages a modern, serverless ecosystem optimized for AI integration, cryptographic security, and rapid deployment:Core Framework: Next.js (React) App Router for unified frontend rendering and backend route handlers, enabling seamless execution of server-side logic.Language: TypeScript, providing robust end-to-end type safety.
